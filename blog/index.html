Who Guards the Guard? Reverse Firewalls That Survive a Hardware Trojan

TERRA · ESORICS 2025

Reverse firewalls defend you against your own subverted machine. But the firewall is hardware too, bought from somebody. A Trojan inside it quietly restores the covert channel the firewall was built to close — so we redefine the guarantee, rebuild the firewall out of secret-shared components, and put the result on a Raspberry Pi.

Shrinking the Keys: Compact p-Party FSS with a Non-linear Decoder

IACR Communications in Cryptology, Vol. 1(2)

Function secret sharing is beautifully cheap for two servers and painfully expensive for more. By moving to seed-homomorphic PRGs and letting the decoder compare rather than only add, the keys shrink from 32 MB to about 4 MB — and the comparison function becomes small enough to put thousands of them in a neural-network layer.
